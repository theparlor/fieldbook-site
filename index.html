In the world

Your receipts, turned into a defensible expense report.

Fieldbook is a composable expense lifecycle for road-warrior consultants. Drop receipts in an inbox. Get a policy-audited, narratively coherent, submission-ready package back — without touching a spreadsheet. The pipeline knows the difference between a 1099 contractor's true cost of travel and an FTE's reimbursement claim.

The problem it solves

Expense reports aren't an admin task. They're a compliance problem.

The real cost of a bad expense report isn't time — it's trust. A missing receipt, a policy violation, a tip over the cap: any of these can delay reimbursement, trigger a flag, or erode credibility with the client you're billing. Expensify and Concur handle capture. They don't understand your engagement's policy version, can't build a narrative around the true cost of showing up, and don't know whether you're a 1099 or a W-2.

The pipeline doesn't just file receipts. It builds the defensible record that earns reimbursement without a follow-up email.
How the pipeline works

Five stages. One append-only spine. Zero manual matching.

→

INTAKE

Inbox scan, receipt classification, OCR, and filing. A five-strategy matcher handles hotels, card exports, PDFs, and plain images. High-confidence items auto-commit; uncertain ones get flagged for review.

→

LEDGER + COMPLY

Receipts land in an append-only engagement ledger — never sliced by month. Compliance runs per-transaction, not per-type: alcohol, tips, daily caps, receipt status. Every flag cites policy section and version.

→

NARRATE + EXPORT

The narrative adapts to who's submitting. A 1099's True Cost of Travel model (nights away, rate erosion, domestic offset) is different from an FTE's gap analysis. Export is a projection from the ledger — CSV, multi-sheet workbook, Projector PSA format.

What makes it different

The ledger is the spine. Export is always a view.

The architectural principle the whole product is built around

The ledger is append-only. Everything else is a projection.

Most expense tools treat the monthly report as the record. Fieldbook treats the ledger as the source of truth and the report as a filtered view of it. Switching from weekly to monthly submission is a parameter change. Running a compliance audit against last quarter's data is a query, not a rebuild. The ledger never gets sliced, never gets deleted from, never gets duplicated into a report — every export is generated from it on demand. Two outputs distinguish what the system knows from what the person needs to know:

Compliance audit

What the policy engine found — per-transaction flags with citation, severity, and a binary pass/fail for the submission package.

Narrative model

What the person submitting should say — persona-dependent, citing the real cost of engagement travel in language that earns approval.

Behind the veil

How Fieldbook was forged.

Fieldbook wasn't designed in a slide deck. It was born from a real March 2026 expense cycle — Brien's Subaru/Turnberry engagement — and generalized when the patterns became clear. It was built, and is still governed, by the same machinery that produced every product in this portfolio.

The operating model

Intent

Fieldbook carries its own .intent/ governance — signals, specs, decisions, traced in git. The append-only ledger contract is a ratified spec. The compliance engine cites policy section and version because the spec required it. This product is in the world precisely because the Intent loop closed on real data before the pipeline shipped.
